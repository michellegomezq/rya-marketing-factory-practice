Voice And Tone: Rya Rightsizing

Source key: (BB) brand book · (MP) marketing plan · [TBC] = not confirmed by the sources


The test for every line: Would this help a worried adult child, or their parent, feel calmer and more capable? If not, rework it. (BB)
The voice (constant)
Compassionate · Reassuring · Respectful · Clear and practical · Hopeful (BB)
"We" for Rya, "you" for the reader.
Short-to-medium sentences. Grade 7–8 reading level.
Canadian spelling.
Exclamation marks rarely.
No emoji in service copy; sparing on social. [TBC: what "sparing" means in practice]
Write like a calm, capable friend who shows up with a plan, a kind word and packing tape. (BB)
1. Brand personality: this, not that
Warm, not syrupy. Kind and personal, without gushing or over-sweet language.
Capable, not clinical or bossy. We bring a plan and clear next steps, and the client still makes the decisions.
Respectful, not condescending. Older adults are capable and in charge of their own move. We never talk down to them or about them.
Grounded, not alarmist. We name the strain of a move honestly, but we don't amplify fear or pressure anyone to act.
Hopeful, not falsely cheerful. We point to calm and manageable order, and we don't pretend a hard chapter is easy.
Also in the brand book: community-minded, never corporate. Held in reserve as a sixth trait, not included here to keep the list to five. (BB)
2. Tone by situation
The voice stays the same. The tone flexes. (BB)
Situation	Tone	What it looks like
First contact (inquiry, consultation, website, ads)	Warm, inviting, no pressure (BB)	Speak to the adult child first, who is often busy, far away and worried, and make the senior feel included. Make one clear offer: "Book your complimentary consultation, in-home or virtual." No urgency CTAs. (BB)
Educational post (blog, FAQ, carousel, guide)	Helpful, encouraging, expert (BB)	Answer one real question in plain language. Explain rightsizing versus downsizing where there's room: "We call it rightsizing, because it's not about having less." (BB) Don't promise services Rya doesn't provide. (MP)
Follow-up (after a consultation or inquiry, thank-yous, partner follow-ups)	Sincere, personal, brief (BB)	Thank them, say what happens next, and leave the next step with them. Don't chase. [TBC: tone for real estate and community partners; the sources give no tone for them beyond "adapt to each audience" (MP)]
Sensitive moment (grief, estates, loss; very full homes)	Quiet, tender, patient (grief, estates); non-judgmental, steady, practical (very full homes) (BB)	Slow down and use fewer words. Acknowledge what the belongings mean before offering logistics. No labels, no humour, no sales push. Offer practical help in small steps. Estate line from the brand book: "Kind, organized help clearing a loved one's home, with as little as possible going to landfill."
Also in the brand book, not requested here: pricing and process (straightforward, transparent, calm); adult child at a distance (reassuring, efficient, communicative); social media (friendly, lightly upbeat, local). (BB)
Adapt by audience (MP): for adult children, stress convenience and less stress. For older adults, stress dignity and independence.
3. Words to use
Core terms: rightsizing (primary term for what Rya does) · downsizing (secondary; good for search, in titles, headings, meta descriptions, URLs and hashtags, and alongside rightsizing: "rightsizing and downsizing")
Offer language: complimentary consultation, in-home or virtual · three-hour blocks of time or more · blocks of time · investment · family liaison
People and things: seniors · older adults · your parent · belongings · heirlooms
Feeling and approach: next chapter · at your pace · meaningful · dignity · peace of mind · honour · support · calm · manageable · thoughtful · compassionate · guide
Naming: "Rya Rightsizing" in full on first reference, then "Rya." (BB)
Place: North & West Vancouver and the Sea to Sky corridor. Hashtag: #NorthAndWestVancouver. (BB)
Signature phrases (from the website): Guided by heart. Grounded in compassion. · Making space for what matters. · From overwhelming clutter to manageable, meaningful order. · You don't have to navigate this alone. (BB)
Replacements (MP): "belongings," "donate," and "thoughtfully organize."
"+": fine in taglines and short headers. Use "and" in body copy. (BB)
Primary tagline: Compassionate rightsizing, downsizing + move services for seniors. (BB)
4. Words to avoid
About belongings: junk · purge · get rid of · throw away · clean out · "stuff" in formal copy · cluttered mess. ("Junk removal" is fine only when naming that kind of service, for example a competitor type.) (BB, MP)
About people: elderly · "old folks" · hoarder as a label (BB)
About price and offer: free consultation (say "complimentary consultation, in-home or virtual") · 3-hr minimum / minimum · sessions (say "three-hour blocks of time or more") · cheap (BB)
About commitment: no long-term contract · all-or-nothing (BB)
About urgency: fast / quick · "before it's too late" · any urgency or fear-based selling (BB)
About place: Metro Vancouver · plain "Vancouver" for the service area (BB)
Name misspellings: RYA · Rya's Rightsizing · Rya Right Sizing (BB)
Claims we can't make yet: no published pricing, discounts or guarantees without Lisa's approval. Never publish the number of clients served to date. (MP)

5. Two before-and-after examples
Example 1: first contact (ad or social post)
Before:
Don't wait until it's too late! Get rid of the junk fast with our free consultation.
After:
When you're ready, we can help you sort, donate and organize your parent's belongings at their pace. Book your complimentary consultation, in-home or virtual.
What changed: removed urgency, "junk," "get rid of," "fast" and "free." Added the primary CTA, "at their pace," and "belongings."
Example 2: educational post (blog or FAQ opening)
Before:
Downsizing is all about getting rid of the stuff you don't need so you can fit into a smaller home.
After:
We call it rightsizing, because it's not about having less. It's about finding the right balance between your space, your belongings, and the life you're living right now.
What changed: leads with "rightsizing," drops "getting rid of" and "stuff," and reframes the move around what matters. The "After" uses Rya's own signature wording from the brand book.
